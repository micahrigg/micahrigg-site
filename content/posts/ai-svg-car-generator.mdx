While on vacation this summer I started a small side project when nothing else was going on in the evenings. I decided to see how far I could get creating my own SVG generator for laser cutting. I currently have an XTool D1 Pro 20 watt diode laser. It does a great job making quick work of thinner materials such as 3mm plywood or 3mm hardboard. With the extensions I also have plenty of room for larger work pieces. 

## The Idea

I've always thought it would be cool to us 3D printing and laser cutting to build a car someday. The idea came to me during some downtime to see if I could use Claude to make a generator that could output laser cut files for a toy car.

## Why SVG?

SVG is a vector graphic format that works well when imported into laser cutting software. Since it is mostly just math it is also easy to scale and change using a set of parameters.

## Working With AI

### What I Asked For

At the time Claude Fable 5 had just come out and I wanted to give it a try with something a little more complex. My prompt included instructions that the output should be to SVG. I also let it know what parameters I wanted such as width, length, height, number of slices, etc. Refinements included adding options for the roundness of corners, belt line/roof line ratios, wheel arch size, and axle height.

### Challenges

While the overall process worked great and produced what I asked in retrospect I made the scope too big when asking for multiple body styles. I would have been able to dial in things more efficiently if I had chosen one style. Once things looked good I could have spun off other branches with different body styles.

## From Screen to Laser

When we returned from vacation I fired up the laser for a first test cut. Surprisingly, it worked the first time around! The parts fit together as I hoped they would without modifications.

## What I Learned

AI agents can go beyond websites and can be used to create tooling for physical items that can be created on CNC machines. This opens up a whole new world of possibilities for small shop manufacturing.

Stay tuned! When I have time I would like to revisit this and add in extra refinements.
